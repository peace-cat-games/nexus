### 0. High-level summary

Nexus is a perfect-information, deterministic, two-player game played on an **n×n grid of intersections**, similar to Go.

It combines:

* **Go-style captures and liberties**
* **Tic-Tac-Toe-style line completion (rows/cols/diagonals)**
* **An economic “debt” layer**: capturing incurs debt that must be resolved when a line is completed
* **Bankruptcy**: if you can’t pay your debt in stones, you lose

2. Move legality (Go rules)
3. Captures and debt tracking
4. Line completion detection (rows/cols/2 main diagonals)

6. Rule 6: forced unblocking when no legal moves exist
7. A simple public API for interacting with the game

## 1. Game rules (for implementation)

### 1.1 Board and players

* Board: **n × n grid of points** (use 0-based indices: rows 0..n-1, cols 0..n-1).
* Two players: `BLACK` and `WHITE`.
* Stones are placed on intersections (points), not inside squares.

Represent colors as an Enum or simple ints (`0` = empty, `1` = black, `2` = white) — your choice, but keep it clear.

### 1.2 Groups, liberties, and captures (Go rules)

* A **group** is a maximal connected set (4-connected: up, down, left, right) of stones of the same color.
* A **liberty** is an empty point adjacent (4-connected) to a stone or group.

When a stone is placed:

1. Place the stone tentatively.
2. Check all **adjacent opponent groups**:

   * If any such group has **no liberties**, that group is captured: all stones in that group are removed.
3. Then check the newly placed stone’s group:

   * If it has no liberties *and* no opponent stones were captured by this move, the move is **illegal (suicide)** and must be rejected.
   * If it has no liberties but the move captured something, it’s **legal** (like in Go).

**Capturing and debt:**

* Let `p1` be a positive integer parameter (e.g. `p1 = 1` initially).
* Each enemy stone captured in this way adds **`p1` units of debt** to the capturing player’s debt counter.

So if Black captures 3 White stones in a single move and `p1 = 1`, Black’s debt increases by 3.

Maintain two integers:

* `debt_black`
* `debt_white`

Always non-negative.

### 1.3 Lines and Completion condition

Define **lines** exactly like Tic-Tac-Toe, but on an n×n board:

* Each **row** (fixed row index, all columns)
* Each **column** (fixed column index, all rows)
* The **two main diagonals** only:

  * Top-left to bottom-right
  * Top-right to bottom-left

A line is **owned** by a player if **all n points** along that line are occupied by that player’s stones.

A **Completion event** happens when, *after a player’s move has been fully applied (including captures)*, that player has **at least one full line**.

Call that player **P** (the one who just moved and triggered Completion), and the other player **Q**.

1.4 Debt resolution when Completion occurs

When a Completion event occurs for player P (the one who just formed at least one full line), we perform a debt audit and resolution. This may result in an immediate win, an immediate bankruptcy loss for P, or the game continuing after P removes some of their own stones.

Let:

D_P = P’s current debt (debt_black or debt_white)

D_Q = Q’s current debt (the other player)

Compute net = D_P - D_Q

Think of this as canceling both sides’ debt; only the difference matters.

Case 1: net <= 0

P’s debt is not greater than Q’s.

All effective debt cancels.

P wins immediately.

The game is terminal, result = BLACK_WIN or WHITE_WIN.

Case 2: net > 0

P has more debt than Q and must “pay” the excess by removing their own stones.

Let k = net.
P is required to remove exactly k of P’s own stones from the board.

If P has fewer than k stones on the board:

P cannot pay the debt without wiping themselves out.

P loses immediately by bankruptcy (opponent wins).

If P has at least k stones:

P chooses which k of their stones to remove.

In rules terms: the player selects the stones that minimize damage to their position.

In engine terms: you must support a way to specify these coordinates (e.g. as a parameter or a separate method). The rules do not require the engine to auto-pick “least damaging” stones; that’s a strategy/AI problem.

Remove those k stones (just erase them — no new captures, no extra debt).

After removal:

Recompute whether P still has at least one full line.

If yes, P wins immediately.

If no, the game continues:

Debts are now considered settled; reset both debts to zero:

debt_black = 0, debt_white = 0

The board stays as modified.

Turn passes to Q (the other player).

Important:

Only P ever removes stones during Completion resolution.

Q never removes stones as part of the Completion step.

The “least damaging” choice is a strategic choice by the player/AI, not something the core rules engine must optimize.

### 1.5 Rule 6: No legal move → Forced Unblocking

On a player’s turn, before they can lay a stone normally, we must check if they have **any legal placement move** (using Go rules and ignoring Completion for the moment).

* If they **do** have at least one legal placement: they must choose a legal placement as normal.
* If they **have no legal placement move**:

  * They must perform **Forced Unblocking**.

#### Forced Unblocking (concept)

* Let current player be P, opponent be Q.
* P must remove the **minimum number** of Q’s stones needed such that *after* removing those stones, there exists at least **one legal placement** for P.

In this implementation:

* You don’t have to explore *all* subsets combinatorially.
  A reasonable deterministic approach is fine, such as:

  * Scan all single Q-stones: for each, simulate removing it and see if any legal move appears.

    * If yes, choose the lexicographically smallest such stone as the set S (size 1).
  * If none exist, scan all pairs of Q-stones, etc.
  * For small boards and test purposes, you can search in increasing cardinality until you find a solution, and fail if none exist.
* The key property: you must guarantee that the chosen set S is of **minimum size** (no smaller set could work).

Let `k = |S|` = number of Q’s stones removed for unblocking.

* Remove those `k` stones of Q.
* For each stone removed in Forced Unblocking, P incurs **p₂** units of debt:

  * `debt_P += k * p2`.
* After removing them, now P proceeds to make a **normal move**:

  * A regular placement, with Go legality, capture checks, and then possible Completion resolution.

If **no such set S exists** (i.e., even removing all opponent stones wouldn’t allow any legal move):

* P loses immediately (cannot move or unblock).

### 1.6 End of game conditions

The game ends when:

* A player wins via Completion + debt resolution; or
* A player loses by **bankruptcy** (cannot pay net debt or has to remove more stones than they have); or
* A player loses because they have no legal move *and* Forced Unblocking is impossible.

Once the game is terminal, further moves should raise an error or be rejected.

### 1.7 Ko / repetition

For this first implementation:
**Do NOT implement superko or position repetition rules.**

You may allow repeated positions; tests will not rely on ko-like edge cases.

## 2. Code design requirements

Implement Nexus as a small, testable Python module.

    * `test_forced_unblocking.py`

At minimum, one file for the engine and one test file is fine, but keep code tidy.

### 2.2 Core classes / functions (minimum API)

Implement something along these lines:

```python
class Player(Enum):
    BLACK = 1
    WHITE = 2

class GameResult(Enum):
    ONGOING = 0
    BLACK_WIN = 1
    WHITE_WIN = 2

@dataclass
class GameState:
    board: List[List[int]]  # 0 = empty, 1 = black, 2 = white
    size: int
    to_move: Player
    debt_black: int
    debt_white: int
    result: GameResult
```

Essential methods (on either `GameState` or a `NexusGame` class):

1. `legal_moves(self) -> List[Tuple[int, int]]`

   * Return list of (row, col) where a normal placement is legal for `to_move` (Go legality only; ignore Completion and Rule 6 here).

2. `apply_move(self, row: int, col: int) -> None`

   * Applies a **full move** for `to_move`:

     * If there are any legal moves, `row, col` must be one of them (otherwise raise).
     * Place stone, resolve captures, update debt for captures.
     * Check for Completion; if triggered, perform debt resolution and possibly end game.
     * If no Completion win/loss, pass turn to the other player.

3. `step_with_forced_unblocking(self, row: int, col: int) -> None`

   * High-level "do a turn":

     * If there is at least one legal move, behave like `apply_move(row, col)`.
     * If there are no legal moves:

       * Run Forced Unblocking (Rule 6) as described.
       * After removing opponent stones and adding `p2` debt, ensure that `(row, col)` is now a legal move; then place the stone and continue as normal (captures, Completion check, etc).
       * If Forced Unblocking is impossible, mark loss for current player.

   Alternatively: you can have a single `make_move(row, col)` that internally handles both cases.

4. Helper methods (can be private):

   * `_find_group(self, r, c) -> Set[Tuple[int,int]]`
   * `_group_liberties(self, group) -> Set[Tuple[int,int]]`
   * `_capture_groups_if_needed(...)`
   * `_count_lines_for_player(player) -> int`
   * `_has_full_line(player) -> bool`
   * `_resolve_completion(player) -> None` (may update result, debts, and board)
   * `_forced_unblock_if_needed() -> Optional[List[Tuple[int,int]]]`

     * Returns list of removed stones, or raises if impossible.

Make sure the API is **clean enough to be used by an AI later**.

## 3. Unit tests (MUST implement)

Write clear unit tests that verify core behavior.

### 3.1 Test: basic placement and Go legality

* Creating a new `GameState` with size `n = 5`:

  * Board should be empty.
  * `to_move` should be `Player.BLACK`.
  * `result` should be `ONGOING`.
  * `debt_black` and `debt_white` both 0.

* Placing a stone on an empty intersection should:

  * Be legal.
  * Not change debt.
  * Not immediately end the game (no line yet).

* Suicide move:

  * Construct a small situation where placing a stone would create a group with no liberties and no captures (classic Go suicide position).
  * Ensure that `apply_move` (or `make_move`) **rejects** this move (raise an exception or return an error).

### 3.2 Test: simple capture and debt

* On a 3×3 or 5×5 board, set up a position where:

  * White has a single stone surrounded by Black in a way that Black’s next move will capture it.
* After Black plays that move:

  * The White stone should be removed.
  * Black’s debt should increase by `p1` (assume `p1 = 1` in tests).
  * No Completion happens yet and `result` remains `ONGOING`.

### 3.3 Test: simple line completion with zero debt

* On a small board (3×3):

  1. Let `p1 = 1, p2 = 2`.
  2. Play a sequence of moves where:

     * Black fills an entire row (e.g., row 0) with no captures at all.
  3. At the moment Black completes the row:

     * Black’s debt = 0, White’s debt = 0.
     * Completion should trigger.
     * Since `net = 0`, Black should **win immediately**.
     * `result` == `BLACK_WIN`.

### 3.4 Test: completion with higher debt and enough stones to pay

* Set up a position (maybe directly, skipping move sequence) where:

  * It is Black’s turn.
  * Board size 3×3 or 4×4.
  * Black currently:

    * Has **one full line** already, or will create it with the next move.
    * Has debt `debt_black = 3`.
  * White has `debt_white = 1`.

* Let Black make the move that completes a line (if not already complete).

Then on Completion:

* `net = debt_black - debt_white = 2`.
* Black must remove exactly 2 of their stones.
* Ensure your implementation removes 2 Black stones deterministically.
* After removal:

  * Arrange the test so that the line is **broken** (no longer full).
* Expected:

  * No immediate win or loss from Completion.
  * Both debts reset to 0.
  * Game is still `ONGOING`.
  * Next `to_move` is White.

### 3.5 Test: bankruptcy on completion

* Set up a position where:

  * It’s Black’s turn.
  * If Black plays at a certain point, they complete a full line.
  * `debt_black` is significantly higher than `debt_white` so that:

    * `net = debt_black - debt_white > number_of_black_stones_on_board`.

* When Black makes that move:

  * Completion triggers.
  * Black must remove `net` of their stones, but they don’t have enough.
  * Implementation should detect this and declare **Black loses by bankruptcy**.
  * `result == WHITE_WIN`.

### 3.6 Test: forced unblocking (Rule 6)

Construct a small example where:

* It’s Black’s turn.
* Every empty intersection would be suicide for Black **if** placed directly (i.e., `legal_moves()` returns empty).
* But there exists at least one White stone such that removing it would create at least one legal Black move.

Test:

* Call a high-level function (e.g., `make_move`) that knows to:

  * Detect `no legal moves`.
  * Run Forced Unblocking.
  * Remove the minimal number of White stones (likely 1 in this test).
  * Increase Black’s debt by `p2 * removed_count`.
  * Then allow Black to place a stone on the specified point.
* Assert:

  * The White stone(s) were removed.
  * Black’s debt increased appropriately.
  * The subsequent Black move is legal.
  * The game is still `ONGOING` unless this move also triggers Completion (if you want, you can make a test where it does).

### 3.7 Test: illegal move when game already ended

* After a win is recorded (via Completion or bankruptcy),

  * Any attempt to call `make_move` / `apply_move` should raise an error or no-op with clear behavior.
  * Test that this is enforced.
